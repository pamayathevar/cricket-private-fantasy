Supported league formats

Owned / auction

League players may have one league owner. Other owners can borrow them subject to the configured deductions and special-player rules.

All-open

Ownership, bids and other-owner deductions are disabled. All eligible league players are available to every owner.

Royalty / Unique variants

Royalty and Unique restrictions are independent switches with versioned formulas, scope and effective fixtures.

Core league rules

05

Playing XI

Transfers

Configured periods

IPL 2026 uses 105 league-stage transfers for M1–70 and 4 playoff transfers for M71–74. The first actual submission in each configured period is free.

What counts

The current submitted player IDs are compared with the latest prior valid submitted XI. Adding a different player counts; changing C/VC/BAI/BOI alone does not.

Own players

Using or carrying an unchanged player does not create an extra transfer. Transfer accounting is lineup-difference based, not ownership-fee based.

Authoritative result

The submission RPC serializes the chain and returns the server-calculated transfer count; the UI never supplies the final charge.

Acquisition and roster

  • One player can have at most one owner inside a league; an open player has no league owner.
  • Selection cost drives XI budget. Auction bid price is stored separately and must not replace selection cost.
  • New or edited player selection cost cannot be lower than the current minimum selection cost already used by that IPL league pool.
  • Inactive players remain in history but cannot enter a new valid XI.
  • League format and competition-defining settings lock after play begins unless an explicit versioned workflow permits a future effective fixture.

Behavior under change

Critical workflows and edge cases

06
Submit or resubmit an unlocked XI
Load the editing fixtureThe screen derives the carried XI, the latest saved submission and its valid baseline.
Edit and validateEleven players, budget, roles, team cap, special restrictions and booster target are checked.
Warn about cascadeIf later unlocked XIs are submitted, the owner is told that resubmitting an earlier match will reset those saved teams.
Submit transactionallysubmit_lineup_with_transfer_result locks the relevant chain, persists the XI and returns its authoritative transfer count.
Reset later unlocked submissionsThey are removed/refunded and will carry forward from the newly resubmitted XI. Locked submissions are never silently rewritten.
Fixture lock and lineup reveal

At the lock, no new client mutation can change the XI. Submitted teams become eligible for league-wide reveal. A skipped owner carries the rules-defined prior state but does not create a submission or consume a free submission benefit. Scores and standings remain pending until publication.

No Result / abandoned match

An administrator settles the fixture using settle_no_result_match. The match contributes zero fantasy points and no match ranking. Transfers and a consumed booster for that fixture are refunded, and that XI is removed from the carry-forward chain.

Two-match-day edge case

If M4 becomes No Result after M5 has already locked, M5 remains fixed. Its transfer charge is recalculated against the latest prior valid XI — M3 in this example. If M5 is still unlocked, it is reset and carries M3 instead.

Points engine

Scoring, boosters, royalty and Unique

07

Scoring values are configuration, not universal constants. A published fixture resolves an effective scoring version so historical totals stay reproducible.

Explainable point components

  • Batting: runs, boundaries, milestone, strike-rate and duck adjustments.
  • Bowling: wickets, wicket type/bonus, maiden and economy adjustments.
  • Fielding: catches, stumpings and run-out participation.
  • Bonus: configured match/participation awards.
  • Lineup multipliers: C, VC, BAI, BOI and 3X target.
  • Special adjustments: Unique deductions and separately credited ROY.

Booster inventory

CodeEffectDefault use
3XTriple one targeted player.Once across all phases.
2UPDouble the submitted XI total.Once in Phase 1 and once in Phase 2.
SUP-TRSuper Transfer treatment for the fixture.Once across all phases; hidden/rejected on a free lineup.

Only one booster can be selected per match. It never carries automatically.

Royalty

When a borrower uses another owner's player, the owning member receives ROY only when the owner also submitted that same player in their own XI for the fixture. The borrower keeps the player's full fantasy contribution. Regular royalty is the greater of 5% or 5 points; marquee royalty is the greater of 15% or 15 points. Each borrower credit is rounded to a whole point, is non-negative and is stored separately from fantasy performance.

Automatic Unique qualification

Counted

Borrowed appearances in locked, scored XIs where that player's current IPL team is playing in the fixture.

Excluded

Use by the owner, fixtures not involving the IPL team, No Result fixtures, unlocked drafts and unscored submissions.

Threshold

Default limit is 56 qualifying uses. The player becomes Automatic Unique on the 57th use, effective from the next match.

A borrower cannot assign restricted power roles to a Unique player; the owner may. Marquee status remains a separate label and rule path.

Migration rule

Every production schema or function change must be a new file under supabase/migrations, paired with a read-only verifier when applicable. Never leave an untracked dashboard-only database change as the production source of truth.

Trust boundaries

Security and permissions

09

Authentication

Supabase email OTP. The Expo client persists only the authenticated session using AsyncStorage and a process lock.

Authorization

RLS policies scope reads/writes to active league memberships. Admin RPCs verify league_admin server-side.

Secrets

EXPO_PUBLIC_* variables are public. The client uses only the Supabase public/publishable key; never a service-role key.

Role matrix

OperationOwnerLeague adminServer / trusted operation
Read own league stateAllowed by membershipAllowed—
Submit own unlocked XIAllowed via wrapper RPCAllowed for own owner entryValidates all rules and transfer chain
Edit league player / replacementDeniedAllowed via audited RPCEnforces current minimum cost
Publish league rulesDeniedAllowed before lock/effective boundaryCreates versioned configuration
Publish match scoresDeniedAllowed through safe wrapperCompleteness checks and atomic totals
Settle No ResultDeniedAllowed through settlement RPCRefund/rebase/reset transaction

Security controls to retain

  • RLS on every API-exposed table and security-invoker standings views.
  • Direct grants removed from lower-level submission/publication functions; client calls safe wrapper RPCs.
  • Tracked-secret scan in the release gate; signing credentials and provider secrets never enter Git.
  • Audit events for member, rule, ownership, player and score-publication changes.
  • Parameterized Supabase queries/RPCs; do not construct SQL in the client.

Mobile delivery

Expo and EAS release model

13

Expo is the application runtime/toolchain. EAS is Expo's cloud build and release service: it compiles the JavaScript/TypeScript project into signed native Android/iOS artifacts without requiring the final build to happen on this Mac.

1Development

npm start runs Metro for rapid iteration. A simulator, Expo-compatible client or web browser connects to the local bundle.

2Preview

EAS creates a signed internal-distribution APK. Install it directly on Android devices for QA; it is not a Play Store release.

3Production

EAS creates a signed AAB. Upload/submit it to Google Play internal testing, then promote through closed/open/production tracks.

Environment variables

Both EAS preview and production environments require:

EXPO_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL=https://<project>.supabase.co
EXPO_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_KEY=<public-or-publishable-key>
Never put privileged secrets in EXPO_PUBLIC variables

These values are compiled into the app and can be inspected by users. A Supabase service_role key, database password, Android keystore or provider credential must not be placed there or committed.

Release sequence

  1. Pull the intended commit and confirm the worktree is clean.
  2. Run npm ci and npm run check:production.
  3. Confirm EAS preview/production variables point to the intended Supabase environment.
  4. Build preview APK, install on physical Android devices and complete QA acceptance.
  5. Tag/record the accepted commit and create a production AAB.
  6. Submit to Google Play internal testing; verify install, sign-in and production backend connectivity.
  7. Promote gradually. Monitor authentication, Supabase errors, score publication and crash reports.
APK versus AAB

An APK is convenient for direct installation and QA. An AAB is the Google Play publishing format; Play generates optimized APKs for each device. A successful EAS build does not by itself publish the app.

Runbook

Operations, incident handling and rollback

14

Before a match

  • Fixture teams, time and lock state verified.
  • Current playing/scoring/special rule versions published.
  • Admin and owner access tested.
  • Score import template/provider mapping ready.

After a match

  • Score facts and player mappings complete.
  • Point breakdown reviewed against source.
  • Safe publication succeeds atomically.
  • Winner, standings, ROY and player history spot-checked.

No Result

  • Confirm official cancellation/abandonment.
  • Settle once through the dedicated RPC.
  • Verify refunds, future unlocked resets and locked rebase.
  • Communicate affected owner changes.

Scoring correction

  • Record source/evidence and affected fixture.
  • Correct staged facts, not ad-hoc totals.
  • Republish through the safe workflow.
  • Confirm audit record and downstream standings.

Rollback model

  • Client: stop promotion and restore the previous accepted Play release. Native rollback does not roll back the database.
  • Database: prefer forward-fix migrations. Destructive schema rollback requires explicit impact analysis because historical XIs/scores must remain reproducible.
  • Rules: publish a future effective version; do not mutate the rule version already used by a completed fixture.
  • Scores: use the correction/republish path with audit evidence; never directly edit visible standings.

Observability baseline

Retain EAS build logs, Supabase database/auth logs, audit_events, score-publication evidence and QA artifacts for every release. Before a wider public launch, add centralized crash/error reporting and product telemetry with privacy review.
